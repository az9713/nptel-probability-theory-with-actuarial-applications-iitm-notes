A study path and notation guide

The central modeling sequence is gross loss, payment per loss, claim count, and aggregate payment. Each stage has its own distribution and assumptions.

Choose the route that matches the question

For a first reading, work through the chapters in order. The dependencies begin with events and conditioning, then introduce distributions, expectation, dependence, transformations, conditional moments, generating functions and approximation. Insurance modifications and aggregate loss use that foundation.

For a focused refresher, start with Chapters 1–4 and test yourself on their exercises. Move to Chapters 7–8 if dependence or conditional laws cause errors. Use Chapters 12–14 before the random-sum chapters if total variance, mixing, or MGF composition is unfamiliar. The original concentration appendix supplies a proved bridge for the missing Week 8 recordings.

For insurance modeling practice, read Chapters 17–20 as a connected sequence. Specify the payment function before integrating it. Calculate first and second moments under the same per-loss or per-payment convention. Only then combine frequency and severity. The final two chapters revisit the full toolchain and work through all thirty mock-examination mechanisms.

The notation contract

SymbolMeaning and convention
\(\Omega, A, \Pr(A)\)Sample space, event, and probability. A portfolio becomes a sample space only after a sampling experiment is specified.
\(X\)Nonnegative ground-up loss before insurance modifications, in currency units.
\(F_X(x), f_X(x)\)CDF and density when a density exists. CDFs also describe discrete and mixed laws.
\(\overline F_X(x)\)Survival probability \(\Pr(X>x)\). Some chapters write this probability explicitly to avoid a clash with aggregate payment \(S\).
\(d\)Ordinary deductible, in the same units as gross loss.
\(u\)Pre-coinsurance layer width. The contract \(Y=c\min((X-d)_+,u)\) has actual payment cap \(cu\).
\(L\)Ground-up ceiling, when that convention is used. A layer from \(d\) to \(L\) has width \(u=L-d\).
\(c\in[0,1]\)Insurer coinsurance fraction, applied to the stated payment layer.
\(Y\)Payment per ground-up loss, including zero payments. A positive-payment conditional variable is defined separately.
\(N,S\)Count and aggregate sum \(S=\sum_{i=1}^NY_i\), with empty sum zero. The count convention must match the severity convention.
\(\alpha,\beta\)Gamma shape and rate; mean \(\alpha/\beta\), variance \(\alpha/\beta^2\). A scale parameter is explicitly labelled when introduced.
\(M_X(t),K_X(t)\)MGF and its logarithm on the domain where the MGF is finite. They need not exist for all severity models.
\(\mu,\sigma^2\)Mean and variance; normal standardization divides by \(\sigma\), the standard deviation.

A symbol may be locally repurposed in a self-contained mathematical example, with its meaning explicitly reintroduced. In particular, \(X,Y\) can denote generic coordinates in joint-distribution chapters. The insurance convention becomes operative when the policy model is introduced.

Use the worked solutions actively

First write a solution with the expandable answer closed. State the assumptions aloud, then derive the result. Open the worked solution only after recording where you became stuck. A derivation mistake and a wrong choice of random variable need different practice.

Run a chapter's Python program and compare its output with the displayed result. Change one model parameter and predict the direction of change before running it again. The exact checks are small enough to inspect line by line. They are finite demonstrations rather than proofs of every general statement.
