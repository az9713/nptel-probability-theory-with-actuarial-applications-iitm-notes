Sources and further reading

The recordings set the coverage. The mathematical arguments in this edition are independently written, with source references attached to related topics.

The primary course sources

  1. Neelesh S. Upadhye, Probability Theory with Actuarial Applications, IIT Madras / NPTEL.The official course identity and attribution. This is an independent educational edition, with no endorsement claimed.
  2. Official course syllabus.Downloaded and read. Its twelve-week plan includes a Week 8 on approximations, inequalities, and tail bounds; no Week 8 lesson recordings appear in the supplied playlist.
  3. The supplied NPTEL-NOC IITM YouTube playlist.The complete preserved inventory appears in the recording map. Available audio was transcribed locally using faster-whisper base.en. Caption retrieval failed, and publisher captions were not used.

Mathematical and actuarial references

  1. Charles M. Grinstead and J. Laurie Snell, Introduction to Probability, author-hosted CHANCE edition.Open primary teaching reference. Relevant chapters cover combinatorics, conditional probability, distributions, expectation, sums, limit theorems, and generating functions. The edition derives its own arguments rather than copying the book's exposition or exercises.
  2. Loss Data Analytics, Open Actuarial Textbooks Project.Community-authored primary actuarial text. The chapters on severity modifications and aggregate-loss models give additional context for deductibles, limits, mixing, and compounding. Precise inspected section headings are cited in the associated insurance chapters.
  3. Hossein Pishro-Nik, Introduction to Probability, Statistics, and Random Processes.Author-hosted open textbook offered as further reading for probability prerequisites. A link here does not assert a cover-to-cover verification or adopt every convention in that book.

Attribution and evidence boundaries

A timestamp link identifies a related discussion in a recording. It does not imply that the lecturer supplied every proof, original example, code listing, or exercise in that section. The chapter source notes preserve this distinction.

Machine transcription can omit a formula, reverse a sign, or recognize a parameter incorrectly. A normalized density, an explicitly stated support, or a complete derivation can resolve a mathematical ambiguity without establishing exactly what appeared on the slide. Remaining source uncertainty is labelled locally and recorded in the source evidence. Comprehensive audiovisual review and formal verification are not claimed.

Original code and the reader build tools are included for inspection. Every published execution record concerns the finite program and input shown. It does not verify an unrestricted theorem or establish the realism of a loss model. The editorial record reports actual independent AI review rounds and their scope; it does not claim human expert certification.

Raw recordings, transcripts, downloaded reference documents, internal author reports, and private editorial reports remain under the unpublished working directory. The public repository contains rewritten notes, source locators, bundled rendering assets, executable examples, and curated validation records.

Reader runtime

Mathematics uses a locally bundled MathJax 3.2.2 SVG renderer, distributed under its Apache license. All reader styles, controls, search data, and rendering assets are local. External recordings and references require an internet connection.
